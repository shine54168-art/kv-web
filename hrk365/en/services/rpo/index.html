Volume hiring and RPO

Filling twenty seats at once is a different engineering problem from filling one. It calls for process, batching and on-the-ground execution — not simply more consultants.

Discuss your hiring plan →

Two ways to engage

Volume hiring project

A single batch with a defined headcount target and completion date.

  • New plants, line expansions or seasonal intakes
  • One standardised brief, screening bar and interview script
  • Assessment days and on-site logistics
  • Weekly reporting on progress, fill rate and drop-off reasons

RPO — recruitment process outsourcing

A longer engagement taking over all or part of your hiring process, working as your external recruitment team.

  • From requisition and CV screening through to interview scheduling
  • Working under your employer brand and inside your ATS
  • Regular hiring metrics and process improvement recommendations
  • Resourcing flexes with volume and duration
What matters

Volume hiring is won on show-up rate, not interview count

Volume hiring reports often look busy — how many invited, how many interviewed — but only two numbers decide the outcome: how many actually started, and how many are still there after three months.

No-shows, accepted offers that never begin, hires who leave in week one — where those losses happen and why has to be tracked before anything can be fixed. Our reporting is built around starts and retention, not activity volume.

The metrics we track

  • Invitation-to-interview attendance rate
  • Offer-to-start conversion
  • Retention at 30 and 90 days
  • Cost and quality by sourcing channel
  • Drop-off reasons — pay, shift, commute, competing offers
