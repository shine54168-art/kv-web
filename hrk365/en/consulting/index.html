HR consulting

Recruitment fills the seat. Consulting keeps it filled.

If the same seat turns over three times a year, the problem is not how fast you hire. We work on the part that recruitment cannot fix.

Discuss a consulting project →
Why we do this

We see the cause from the hiring floor

Search sits in an unusual position: we hear your account and we hear the account of the people who left you. Which managers cannot retain, which grade has drifted below market, what reputation a particular department carries — candidates tell us these things in interviews, and they do not tell you.

Consulting turns those observations into something you can act on. It is not another generic management framework; it is a diagnosis grown out of your own talent flow.

When to bring in a consultant

  • The same seat has turned over three times this year.
  • You pay at market and people still leave.
  • Grades are ad hoc; raises and promotions have no defensible basis.
  • Labour inspections, disputes or overtime calculations worry you.
  • You are scaling fast on an HR system built for twenty people.

Consulting modules

Each runs on its own, or combines into a full organisational review.

Organisation and job architecture review

Re-establishing who does what, what it is worth, and how someone moves up.

  • Role inventory and overlap analysis
  • Grading structure and title system design
  • Promotion paths and career maps
  • Review of how headcount is actually allocated

Pay structure and benchmarking

So that raises and offers have a basis — competitive outside, defensible inside.

  • Salary band design by grade
  • Benchmarking against market data
  • Mix of base, bonus, profit share and equity
  • Internal equity review

Labour compliance review

Surfacing risk before an inspection or a dispute does it for you.

  • Working hours, shift patterns and overtime calculation
  • Employment contracts and work rules
  • Leave management, including statutory annual leave
  • Reasonableness of non-compete and confidentiality clauses

Employee relations and attrition analysis

Finding the real reason people leave, not the line on the resignation form.

  • Structured exit interviews and analysis
  • Retention risk assessment and key-talent mapping
  • Turnover analysis by department and by manager
  • Guidance on handling employee relations cases
How a project runs

Four phases; the timeline depends on scope

01

Discovery

Interviews across leadership, managers and staff, plus your existing organisational, pay and turnover data.

02

Diagnosis

Cross-referencing what we heard with what the data shows, to identify causes rather than symptoms — with market benchmarking where relevant.

03

Design

Workable proposals with cost estimates and a sequence for implementation, marking clearly what is essential and what can wait.

04

Implementation support

Helping roll it out — manager communication and document revisions included. A system nobody uses is worth nothing, however well written.
